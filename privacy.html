Privacy & rights

Your music stays yours

Draft · last updated 10 October 2026

Stakkato is a place to make songs with strangers. That only works if you know exactly what happens to a take once you post it. This page explains it in plain words.

In short

  • You keep the copyright of every take you post. We never buy it from you and we never sell it.
  • Everything you post is public. Anyone can hear your take, play on it, and use it in that jam’s song.
  • A released song belongs to everyone on it. Anyone can share it for free with credits. Making money from it needs a yes from every musician on the song.
  • Your music never trains AI. We don’t use your takes to train models, and we don’t let others do it.
  • You can delete your takes and your account. A song that has already been released keeps your take, with your name on it, unless you ask us to take your name off.

Recording happens in your browser. We only receive a take when you post it or upload it. Takes you keep as a draft never leave your device.

3What everyone can see

Stakkato is public by design. Everything below can be seen by anyone, also without an account, and can show up in search engines:

  • every jam, every take and every song, with the audio;
  • your display name next to everything you posted;
  • what each take was played on, votes and likes in totals.

Your email address is never shown. Don’t put anything in a note or a take that you want to keep private.

4Your takes and your rights

You keep the copyright and any neighbouring rights in your performance. By posting a take you give two permissions. Both are non-exclusive, free and worldwide.

To Stakkato

We may store, copy, stream and process your take (for example to draw a waveform, mix it with other takes, or make a smaller file) and show it on Stakkato and in links to Stakkato. We may also use short clips of public jams to show what Stakkato is, always with your name. This permission ends when your take is deleted, except for songs that were already released (see section 5) and copies in backups, which expire on their own.

To the other musicians

Anyone may, inside Stakkato, play your take, record on top of it, use it in the jam’s song or in their own version, and download the stems of that jam to practise and record their part. That’s what you post a take for.

What nobody may do

  • Take your take out of its jam and use it in other work, outside Stakkato, without your permission.
  • Make money from it without your permission (see section 5).
  • Use it to train AI models. This also goes for us.
To checkThe wording of both permissions, the use of short clips for promotion, and whether the AI clause also needs to be in the general terms (text and data mining opt-out under the EU copyright directive).

5When a jam becomes a song

The person who started a jam is its author. The author picks which takes are in the song and decides when it’s released. Being the author doesn’t make the other takes theirs: every musician on the song keeps the rights in their own take.

While the jam is openTakes can go in and out of the song. You can still delete your take.
Once it’s releasedThe song is final. Everyone on it is credited with their name and instrument.
Outside StakkatoFree sharing with credits is fine. Making money needs a yes from everyone on the song.

Sharing a released song

Anyone may play, share and embed a released song for free, as long as everyone on it is credited and nobody makes money from it. This is like a Creative Commons BY-NC licence.

Making money from a song

Putting a song on streaming services, selling it, or licensing it for a film or an ad needs written agreement from every musician whose take is in the released song. Unless they agree otherwise, the income is split in equal shares between everyone on the song, and the person who started the jam counts as one of them.

Mixes

Someone who uploads a mix of a released song adds their own work to it, but the mix uses everyone’s takes. The same rules apply to it as to the song.

6What you promise us

  • You played or made what you post yourself, or you have the rights to post it. No samples, loops or other people’s recordings you don’t have a licence for.
  • Covers: playing someone else’s song is fine for practising in a jam, but releasing it or making money from it needs a licence from the songwriter.
  • Nothing in your take or note is hateful, sexual or breaks the law.
  • You are at least 16, or you have permission from a parent.

If someone reports a take that breaks these rules or someone’s rights, we may hide or remove it, and close accounts that keep doing it.

To checkThe minimum age (Belgium allows consent from 13 for online services), the notice-and-takedown procedure under the Digital Services Act, and how a rights holder reports a take.

7Deleting takes and your account

  • A take in an open jam: you can delete it at any time. It disappears from the jam and from the song. Takes recorded on top of it stay, but no longer play with yours.
  • A take in a released song: the song stays as it was, because the others on it rely on it. You can ask us to take your name off it, or to replace your name with “anonymous”.
  • Your account: delete it in your settings or email us. We delete your email address and your takes that aren’t in a released song within 30 days. Copies in backups are gone within 90 days.

The author of a jam can also delete takes from their jam. That removes the take from the jam, not from your account history.

9Your privacy rights

Under the GDPR you can ask us to see your data, correct it, delete it, get a copy of it (including your takes as audio files), or stop using it for something you object to. Email [privacy@stakkato.app] and we answer within a month.

If you think we handle your data wrongly, you can complain to the Belgian Data Protection Authority (Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit, dataprotectionauthority.be).

10Cookies and local storage

We only store what Stakkato needs to work: your sign-in, and small settings like which microphone you picked. No advertising cookies, no tracking across other sites. That’s why you don’t see a cookie banner.

11Changes and contact

If we change how we handle your takes or your rights, we tell you by email and on the site at least 30 days before it applies. Changes never apply to songs that were already released.

Questions? Email [privacy@stakkato.app].
